How to play

Choose a path. Survive the pressure. Learn after the outcome.

Cyber Scam Simulator is not a passive learning app. You progress by interacting with calls, texts, emails, social posts and SOC missions. Wrong choices can lead to compromise; correct choices unlock the next challenge.

Choose your learning path

Pick a starting path such as personal scam resistance, workplace fraud, social media scams or SOC training. All paths are available without a payment lock.

Meet your guardian

Your guardian sets the tone and tries to push you into mistakes. They are part coach, part adversary. The point is to make you slow down under pressure.

Play the live contact

Answer the call, reply to the text, inspect the inbox or handle the social post. Each choice changes the route and the attacker’s next move.

Face the consequence

Success and failure screens match the scenario. If you were caught, the game explains exactly how the attacker gained control.

Learn, quiz and level up

Lessons are bite-sized and followed by quizzes. If you answer incorrectly, the game can rotate to another question so you have to learn the concept, not memorise the order.

Game modes

Use the tabs to see how different routes work.

Phone-call simulations

Calls use voice output and on-screen transcript text. The caller may become urgent, friendly, frustrated or authoritative depending on your choices. You decide whether to continue, challenge, verify or stop the call.

Text-message simulations

Texts arrive in a phone-style interface. You can reply, ignore, verify or take an action. The scenario progresses from your response rather than from a static multiple-choice screen.

Email and social simulations

Emails and social posts are presented as realistic screens. Players must inspect the context, spot route control, resist urgency and avoid fake checkout, investment or support paths.

SOC red and blue team training

Small mission briefs set up the operation. Red team tasks explore attacker decision paths in a safe training context. Blue team tasks focus on evidence, containment, reporting and recovery decisions.

What counts as a good move?

✓

Leave the contact’s route

Use a known official channel, saved app or existing contact record. Do not verify through a link, number or reply path supplied by the suspicious message.

✓

Protect one-time codes

Codes, passkeys, recovery phrases and approval prompts are control points. The safe move is usually to stop and verify independently.

✓

Act on evidence

In SOC modes, avoid both panic and inaction. Preserve evidence, contain confirmed paths and document decisions.

If a real scam is happening

If something in the game resembles a live incident, stop playing and use official routes. Do not call back numbers or use links supplied by the suspicious contact.

UK

Contact your bank through the number on your card or app. Report fraud/cybercrime through Action Fraud where applicable. Forward suspicious emails to report@phishing.gov.uk and scam texts to 7726.

US and Europe

US users can report fraud through the FTC and identity theft through IdentityTheft.gov. European users should use their national cybercrime reporting route or consumer advice network.

This website is informational and educational. It is not emergency, legal, financial or cybersecurity incident-response advice. If you are at immediate risk, contact local emergency services or your bank/platform through known official channels.
