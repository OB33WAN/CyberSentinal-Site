About the app

A realistic scam simulator, built as a playable mobile game.

Cyber Scam Simulator helps people practise the moment that matters: the live decision. It recreates scam pressure in a safe sandbox so players can learn from mistakes before criminals use the same tactics in real life.

Why it exists

Most scam education explains warning signs after the fact. Real scams do not arrive as clean quizzes. They arrive as phone calls, text threads, fake support messages, urgent emails, social posts, investment pitches and authority pressure.

The app is designed to make players feel that pressure, choose a route and then receive the lesson after the outcome.

> principle: game first

> route logic: choice creates consequence

> learning: reveal after decision

> safety: never request real secrets

What the game simulates

Scenarios are written for UK, US and European contexts, including language, payment rails, official reporting routes and common social-engineering patterns.

1

Personal scams

Bank calls, parcel texts, account lockouts, one-time code capture, fake recovery support and social media promotions.

2

Workplace scams

Payment-detail changes, supplier impersonation, executive pressure, invoice manipulation and remote-access support traps.

3

SOC training

Small red team and blue team briefs where players investigate, contain, escalate and measure realistic cyber incidents.

Safety boundaries

The game is intentionally sandboxed. It is not a hacking tool and it does not ask for real credentials, real payment details, real recovery phrases or real one-time codes.

Simulated content only

Calls, text messages, emails, social posts, domains and incident routes are fictional training content. The app avoids real attacker infrastructure.

Official help routes

If a scenario resembles something currently happening to a player, they should stop playing and use official reporting routes or contact their bank, platform, employer or local law enforcement through known channels.
